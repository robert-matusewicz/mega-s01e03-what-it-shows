The three problems

parser · month and day flipped by the shared parser. Every tenant on file writes the day first. The shared parser reads month first, so a booking typed as 7 April goes out as 4 July. It hits every channel of every tenant. This is the bug the queued fix is for.

hint · month and day flipped by the booking-v3 form. The booking-v3 form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People type what the hint says, month first, and the form reads it the tenant’s way, day first. Same symptom, other direction, different code: fixing the parser does not touch it. Only booking-v3 tenants, only through the form.

one-day-move · the date moves by one day. No flip. One meeting shows Tuesday in Lisbon and Monday in Toronto; 6 October shows as a Monday; a whole month is one day off. The desk’s notes mark these five reports as a one-day move. The desk counts them where it files them, under the tenant’s cause, and footnotes them there; this page keeps that count and gives them a row and a ticket of their own. A day boundary is being crossed somewhere between the booking’s time zone and the invite’s.

How to tell them apart

  1. Is the date flipped (day and month swapped) or moved by one day? Moved: one-day-move.
  2. Flipped and the report came through the booking form of a booking-v3 tenant: hint.
  3. Flipped, anything else: parser.

Intake’s tag cannot do this. It guesses from the reporter’s browser language, and the browser language says nothing about the tenant’s booking form or channel. The table above counts how often the guess was right.

What changes on the desk

  • Close nothing on #4471 when the parser fix ships. Check the hint and one-day-move tickets separately; their reports will keep coming.
  • Subject lines did not tell these apart, and bodies did. Reading one body per tenant and channel would have split the ticket in April.
